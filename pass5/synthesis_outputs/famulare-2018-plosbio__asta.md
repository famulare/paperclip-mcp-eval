## Prompt 1 — Central mechanistic claim

The central claim, stated in the abstract, is that OPV's capacity to seed vaccine-derived outbreaks is governed by population immunity within a force-of-infection framework: "inactivated polio vaccine alone is sufficient to prevent outbreaks in low force of infection settings, and that OPV can be started and stopped safely in moderate force of infection settings. However, in high force of infection settings, the conditions that support vaccine-derived outbreaks have only been rare because population immunity has been high. With insufficient immunity, the Sabin strains from OPV will be nearly as capable of causing outbreaks as WPV." The mechanistic vehicle is "a multiscale mathematical model of infection and transmission," where "The within-host model describes the effects of vaccination and waning immunity on shedding and dose response" and "The transmission model captures how shedding and dose response interact with sanitation and person-to-person contact patterns to determine the force of infection in communities." The policy conclusion: "while eradication may still be achieved with continued use of Sabin OPV, a new vaccine is needed to secure eradication for all time."

## Prompt 4 — Expert eye

A domain expert reading only this bundle would notice several concrete signals. (1) The abstract frames a *type-2-specific* real-world hook: "After OPV is no longer used in routine immunization, as with the cessation of type 2 OPV in 2016, population immunity will decline" — anchoring the model to the tOPV2 withdrawal event. (2) The dose-response machinery is defined on a specific scale: HID50 is "the dose (measured in CID50) that infects 50% of orally-exposed and immunologically-naive humans," i.e., human infectious dose expressed in cell-culture units — a detail a generic summary would flatten. (3) The claim is conditional on setting: the key qualitative result is that OPV/WPV outbreak equivalence emerges specifically "in high force of infection settings" with "insufficient immunity," and the paper explicitly reconciles this with historical safety — "this conclusion is compatible with the known safety of OPV prior to global cessation." (4) The abbreviation list enumerates OPV formulations (tOPV/mOPV/bOPV) and "cVDPV circulating vaccine-derived poliovirus," and names "UP Uttar Pradesh," hinting at a specific high-force-of-infection field setting used in the analysis. Beyond these abstract-level cues, no methodological detail (fitting procedure specifics, priors, validation) is available in the bundle.

## Prompt 5 — Overclaim risk

The abstract itself bounds the claims, and these bounds are what would be dangerous to drop. The safety conclusion is explicitly *setting-conditional*: OPV "can be started and stopped safely in moderate force of infection settings" and IPV "alone is sufficient to prevent outbreaks in low force of infection settings" — so a generalized "OPV is safe to stop" or "OPV is dangerous" reading overclaims by ignoring the force-of-infection stratification. The OPV≈WPV equivalence is hedged as "nearly as capable" and only "With insufficient immunity" in "high force of infection settings," not universally. The forward-looking conclusion is itself hedged: "while eradication may still be achieved with continued use of Sabin OPV, a new vaccine is needed to secure eradication for all time" — stating the new-vaccine claim as a necessity for permanence, not as an immediate failure of current strategy. Overclaiming any of these as unconditional would misrepresent the paper.
